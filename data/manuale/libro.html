This manual describes how to operate the application. It does not replace the Aircraft Flight Manual, the Operations Manual or the flight school SOPs. The documents produced are an aid to planning: responsibility for the flight remains with the Pilot in Command.

SECTION 1General

1.1Purpose

The application converts the route prepared in ForeFlight and the weather briefing into the two documents carried in flight: the Operational Flight Plan (OFP) and the NAV-FLIGHTPLAN. All performance, fuel and mass figures are computed from the AFM of the selected aircraft; every intermediate result is shown so that it can be checked.

1.2Scope and applicability

  • Aircraft: Reims Cessna “Reims Rocket”, Continental IO-360-D: I-CCAF (FR172J, AFM Edition 3) and I-CCAB (FR172H, AFM Edition 2). The two AFMs differ only in VR, VY, stall speeds and VA, and the club gives a different VX for each (I-CCAF 68 MPH, I-CCAB 75 MPH): the application uses those of the aircraft selected in Step 1.
  • Operations: VFR flight planning. Final reserve is selectable for VFR (30 min) or IFR (45 min).
  • Units: fuel in litres, fuel flow in L/h, speeds in MPH (as in the AFM), distances in NM, times in UTC.

1.5Responsibilities

The PIC is responsible for the correctness of every input, for every cross-check required by this manual, and for the decision to fly. The application does not navigate, does not decide and does not replace the AFM or the AIP.

1.6Data storage

Access is restricted: accounts are created by the administrator (Section 2.1). Flights are stored only when the pilot uses Save or Proceed (Sections 3.0 and 3.8). They are saved to the pilot's account, are available on any device after signing in and are not visible to other users. A copy is kept on the device in use, so work can continue without a connection: changes are sent when the connection is restored.

SECTION 2Data Ingestion – Import Page

2.1Pre-requisites

  1. INPUT the e-mail and password provided by the administrator to sign in. At the first sign-in, replace the temporary password with your own (at least 10 characters). The Dashboard opens (Section 4.5).
  2. PREPARE the route in ForeFlight and export the NavLog (PDF or text), unless an ACV standard route is flown.
  3. DOWNLOAD the weather and NOTAM briefing PDF covering departure, destination, alternate and route, from SkyBrief (link in the Weather Briefing card).
  4. SELECT New flight on the Dashboard, or + in the side bar: the Import page opens. Flights in the side bar goes back without importing.

2.2Procedure

The Import page has four boxes: the ACV standard routes, the ForeFlight NavLog, the Weather briefing and the Status. The route comes either from a standard route or from a NavLog; the weather always from the briefing PDF.

  1. INPUT part of a route name or of a waypoint in the search box A to narrow the list (optional).
  2. SELECT the route card B. The selected card is underlined; SELECT it again to clear it.
  3. VERIFY the track C and the summary D: number of waypoints, distance, Touch & Go aerodrome and the waypoints in order. Show the waypoints opens the waypoint table.
STANDARD ROUTES ACV
The Aeroclub Varese routes, read from the club’s ForeFlight NavLogs: one card per route, with its track, distance and number of waypoints. The track is drawn from the magnetic tracks and distances of the NavLog, without wind; an out-and-back route is a single line. A route with a Touch & Go arrives in the wizard with the T&G aerodrome already ticked (Step 1). Loading a ForeFlight NavLog replaces the selected route.
  1. LOAD the ForeFlight NavLog in the NavLog drop zone A, when no standard route is flown. Load it before the briefing: its ICAO codes are used to sort the briefing.
  2. VERIFY the NavLog summary B: number of waypoints, total distance and the waypoint table. For a return flight the application states that DEP and ARR are the same aerodrome.
NOTE
Only airports, route, magnetic tracks and distances are taken from the NavLog. ForeFlight speeds and fuel figures are not used: the AFM figures take precedence.
  1. SELECT Get the briefing on SkyBrief A if the briefing has not been downloaded yet: SkyBrief opens in a new tab. Search DEP, ARR and ALTN by ICAO and download the PDF.
  2. LOAD the briefing PDF in the briefing drop zone B.
  3. VERIFY the briefing summary C: issue date and time (UTC) and, for each aerodrome, the availability of METAR, TAF and the number of NOTAMs. N/A means not contained in the briefing.
NOTE
METAR, SPECI, TAF and NOTAM are sorted onto DEP, ARR, ALTN and the T&G aerodrome; from there wind, QNH and temperature feed the performance calculation (Step 4).
  1. CONFIRM both STATUS items are green A.
  2. SELECT Continue B to open the wizard.
CAUTION
Skip and enter manually C opens the wizard without imported data. Route, distances and all weather and NOTAM fields must then be entered by hand. A missing or unreadable file does not block the wizard: its data are entered by hand.

SECTION 3Pre-flight Data Entry – Wizard

3.0Wizard navigation

  1. SELECT a step tab B to go to any step, or Back / Next E at the bottom of the page. On Step 8 Next becomes Proceed (Section 3.8).
  2. SELECT in the side bar A: Flights (the Dashboard, Section 4.5), New flight (the Import page) or User manual (this manual, over the page: Close or Esc goes back).
  3. SELECT the theme button C to choose the display (below). It does not affect the documents.
  4. SELECT the menu D for the document preview and the documents (Section 4.2).
  5. SELECT Save F, at the right end of the step tabs, to store the flight in your account at any step. The wizard stays where it is and the button reads Saved for a moment; a dot before Save means that there are changes not yet saved.
CAUTION
The underline marks the step in progress; steps already passed are shown dimmed. This does not mean that a step is complete or correct.
NOTE
Leaving a flight with changes not saved (Flights, New flight, another flight or its documents, Sign out) asks first: Save saves and goes on, Don’t save goes on without saving, Cancel stays. Closing or reloading the browser tab asks with the browser’s own message.

With a mouse or a trackpad the side bar widens while the pointer is over it: the name of each item appears and the icons grow. The page underneath does not move. With touch only, the side bar stays narrow and its icons work directly.

The theme button opens a choice of Night (dark), Day (light) and Match system, which follows the setting of the device. The new theme spreads from the button.

3.2Step 2 – Route & Fuel

All fuel is in litres, fuel flows in L/h, speeds in MPH, as in the AFM; 1 L = 0.72 kg; 174 L (46 USG) usable. At the top of the step the fuel plan adds up Taxi, Trip, Contingency, Alternate, Final reserve, Minimum required, Extra and Block, as printed on the OFP: litres above, minutes below, Block also in kg. It updates with every value of this step.

  2. VERIFY the header A: aircraft, pilot, date, GS, QNH and runway.
  3. RECORD off-blocks and on-blocks times B by hand.
  4. VERIFY the legs C.
  5. RECORD ATO D in flight at each checkpoint.
  6. VERIFY the alternate row E and the fuel calculation box F.

The Aeroclub Varese navlog produces the same navigation log on the flying club form (Aeroclub di Varese, Navigation Log): one PDF of two A4 landscape pages, front and back, to be printed double-sided. It does not replace the NAV-FLIGHTPLAN, which remains the navigation log of the application.

  1. SELECT Download on the Aeroclub Varese navlog row of the Documents page.
  2. VERIFY the main table (right side of the front): checkpoints, NAV AIDS (Ident = callsign, Freq), MC, ALT, DIST and FUEL per leg and remaining, ETE and ETO, as in the NAV-FLIGHTPLAN. Up to 13 checkpoints fit; up to 6 more continue in the left table, above the Dest. Airport row.
  3. VERIFY the alternate (left side of the front): one leg from destination to alternate. The MC is computed from the great circle and the WMM2025 magnetic variation, and is marked as to be verified.
  4. VERIFY the back: weather by aerodrome from the METAR; weight and balance with the take-off fuel; fuel calculation; take-off data (DEP) and landing data (DEST).
  5. RECORD block times, RETO and ATO by hand.
NOTE
Differences from the club form as printed by hand: CLIMB fuel is the AFM fig. 5-6 block (start-up and take-off included), so no rate is given; DESC. is not published in the AFM; APP. is the 5 minutes per aerodrome; the contingency, which the form does not have, is included in MINIMUM FOB and stated in the Note. ASDA (required) is the take-off ground roll plus the landing ground roll. Emax is the best glide speed. ASDA declared, VLO and VLE are left blank; VX is the figure given by the club for each aircraft. All values are whole numbers: distances, leg fuel and masses to the nearest unit; in the fuel calculation the required quantities are rounded up and each total is the sum of the figures printed, so the columns add up. Arms stay in metres with three decimals.
CAUTION
The form is an aid to flight preparation and does not replace the AFM, the SOP or the AIP. Every pre-filled value must be verified by the pilot before use.

4.5Dashboard – Saved flights

The Dashboard is the first page after signing in. The header A greets the pilot by name, with the UTC time, and draws the route network of the home field: the ACV standard routes and the saved flights, with range rings at 25, 50, 100 and 150 NM. The summary tiles B give the next or latest flight, the flights ready to fly, those waiting for weather and the planned distance. New flight opens the Import page (Section 2). While a flight plan is open in the wizard, Back to the open flight plan returns to it. Reloading the page also shows the Dashboard first.

Each saved flight is a strip with the thumbnail of its track C, drawn from the legs of the Navlog, its badge (STANDBY or READY TO FLY), route, date and times.

  1. SELECT Save in the wizard to store the flight, for example while waiting for the weather. It appears on the Dashboard as a standby flight.
  2. LOAD the briefing PDF with Load briefing D on a standby flight to finalise it: it moves to READY TO FLY.
  3. SELECT Edit E to reopen a flight in the wizard.
  4. SELECT Documents F on a READY TO FLY flight to open its Documents page (Section 4.1).
  5. LOAD a newer briefing with Update WX G to update the weather of a READY TO FLY flight.
NOTE
Opening a saved flight or starting a new one closes the flight plan open in the wizard: with changes not saved, the application asks first whether to save them (Section 3.0).
CAUTION
Saved flights are stored in your account. On a shared device, select Sign out at the end of the session: it removes the local copy from the device. Graphic weather charts are not stored with the flight.

5.4Navigation data

  • Cruise GS is TAS (still air) until corrected for wind by the pilot.
  • The alternate distance is a great-circle distance.
  • Frequencies are the number only, from OurAirports: CROSS-CHECK with current charts / AIP.
  • Navlog times and fuel are recomputed by the application; their totals equal the Trip.

5.5Daylight check

  • Sunrise / sunset: upper limb at −0.833°; civil twilight: centre at −6° (night as defined in Regulation (EU) 923/2012).
  • Rounded conservatively: sunset and end of twilight to the minute before, sunrise to the minute after.
  • Uses the flight date of Step 8 and the UTC times of Step 1. No elevation correction (as in published tables).

5.6Documents and storage

  • The documents are downloaded from the Documents page and printed from the PDF, with the PDF viewer.
  • They are generated from the flight data at the moment of the download: after any change, download them again.
  • Saved flights are stored in the pilot's account and are visible only to that pilot. On a shared device, Sign out at the end of the session.
  • The A5 NAV-FLIGHTPLAN and the club navlog are separate documents, never part of the OFP.
The documents produced are an aid to planning. Responsibility for the flight remains with the Pilot in Command.
