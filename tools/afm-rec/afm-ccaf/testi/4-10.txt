749 | WING FLAP SETTINGS.
812 | Normal take-offs are accomplished with wing flaps up, full throttle, and 2800
843 | RPM. Reduce power to 25 inches (847 mb) of manifold pressure and 2600 RPM
875 | as soon as practical to minimize engine wear.
936 | Using 10° wing flaps reduces the total distance over an obstacle by approxi-
967 | mately 5 per cent. Soft field take-offs can be performed with 15° flaps by lifting
998 | the airplane off the ground as soon as practical in a slightly tail-low attitude. How-
1029 | ever, the airplane should be leveled off immediately to accelerate to a safe climb
1060 | speed. When departing a soft field with an aft c.g. loading, the elevator trim should
1090 | be adjusted towards the nose down direction to give comfortable stick forces during
1121 | the initial climb.
1182 | If wing flaps are used for take-off, they should be left down until all obstacles
1213 | are cleared rather than retract them in the climb to the obstacle. To clear an ob-
1244 | stacle with wing flaps 10°, the speeds shown in the Take-Off chart in Section V
1275 | should be used. If no obstructions are ahead, a best “flaps up” rate-of-climb speed
1305 | (137 km/h - 74 kts - 85 MPH) would be most efficient with flaps retracted. Flap
1336 | deflections greater than 15° are not recommended for take-off under any circum-
1367 | stances.
