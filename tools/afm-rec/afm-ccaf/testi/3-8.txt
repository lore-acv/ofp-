493 | After engine starting and heavy electrical usage at low engine speeds (such as ex-

618 | rent. If the charging rate were to remain above this value on a long flight, the bat-
649 | tery would overheat and evaporate the electrolyte at an excessive rate. Electronic
681 | components in the electrical system could be adversely affected by higher than nor-
712 | mal voltage if a faulty voltage regulator setting is causing the overcharging. To pre-
744 | clude these possibilities, an over-voltage sensor will automatically shut down the al-
775 | ternator and the over-voltage warning light will illuminate if the charge voltage
806 | reaches approximately 16 volts. Assuming that the malfunction was only momen-
838 | tary, an attempt should be made to reactivate the alternator system. To do this,
869 | turn both sides of the master switch off and then on again. If the problem no longer
902 | exists, normal alternator charging will resume and the warning light will go off. If the
933 | light comes on again, a malfunction is confirmed. In this event, the flight should be
964 | terminated and/or the current drain on the battery minimized because the battery can
995 | supply the electrical system for only a limited period of time. If the emergency oc-
1025 | curs at night, power must be conserved for later use of the landing light and flaps
1057 | during landing.
1117 | INSUFFICIENT RATE OF CHARGE.
1178 | If the ammeter indicates a continuous discharge rate in flight, the alternator is
1210 | not supplying power to the system and should be shut down since the alternator
1241 | field circuit may be placing an unnecessary load on the system. All non-essential
1272 | equipment should be turned “OFF” and the flight terminated as soon as practical.
