421 | DITCHING.
483 | Prepare for ditching by securing or jettisoning heavy objects located in the bag-
514 | gage area, and collect folded coats or cushions for protection of occupant’s face at
546 | touchdown. Transmit Mayday message on 121.5 MHz., giving location and intentions.
608 | (1) Plan approach into wind if winds are high and seas are heavy. With heavy
639 | swells and light wind, land parallel to swells.
670 | (2) Approach with flaps 40° and sufficient power for a 300 ft./min. rate of
701 | descent at 113 km/h - 61 kts - 70 MPH.
732 | (3) Unlatch the cabin doors.
763 | (4) Maintain a continuous descent until touchdown in level attitude. Avoid
793 | a landing flare because of difficulty in judging airplane height over a water sur-
825 | face.
856 | (5) Place folded coat or cushion in front of face at time of touchdown.
886 | (6) Evacuate airplane through cabin doors. If necessary, open window to flood
918 | cabin compartment for equalizing pressure so that door can be opened.
950 | (7) Inflate life vests and raft (if available) after evacuation of cabin. The air-
980 | craft can not be depended on for flotation for more than a few minutes.
1165 titolo | FLIGHT IN ICING CONDITIONS.
1224 | Although flying in known icing conditions is prohibited, an unexpected icing
1254 | encounter should be handled as follows:
1315 | (1) Turn pitot heat switch “ON” (if installed).
1346 | (2) Turn back or change altitude to obtain an outside air temperature that is
1376 | less conducive to icing.
1407 | (3) Pull cabin heat control full out to obtain windshield defroster airflow. Ad-
1437 | just cabin air control to get maximum defroster heat and airflow.
1468 | (4) Increase RPM to minimize ice build-up on propeller blades.
1499 | (5) Plan a landing at the nearest airport. With an extremely rapid ice build-up,
1529 | select a suitable “off airport” landing site.
1559 | (6) With an ice accumulation of 0,5 cm or more on the wing leading edges,
1589 | be prepared for significantly higher stall speed.
1619 | (7) Leave wing flaps retracted. With a severe ice build-up on the horizontal
1650 | tail, the change in wing wake airflow direction caused by wing flap extension
1679 | could result in a loss of elevator effectiveness.
