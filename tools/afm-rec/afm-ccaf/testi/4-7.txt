447 titolo | STARTING ENGINE.
509 | The continuous-flow fuel injection system will start spraying fuel in the intake
540 | ports as soon as the throttle and mixture controls are opened and the auxiliary pump
571 | is turned on. If the auxiliary pump is turned on accidentally while the engine is
601 | stopped, with the throttle open and the mixture rich, solid fuel will collect temporar-
633 | ily in the cylinder intake ports, the quantity depending on the amount of throttle
663 | opening and the length of time the pump has been operating. If this happens, it is
694 | advisable to wait a few minutes until this fuel drains away before starting the engine.
724 | To avoid flooding, be sure you are ready to crank the engine as soon as a steady
755 | fuel flow of 2 to 4 gal/hr (7,8 to 15,5 litres/hr) is obtained.
818 | Engine mis-starts characterized by weak, intermittent firing followed by puffs
848 | of black smoke from the exhaust are caused by over-priming or flooding. This situa-
880 | tion is more apt to develop in hot weather, or when the engine is hot. If it occurs,
911 | repeat the starting routine with the throttle approximately 1/2 open, the mixture in
943 | idle cut-off and the auxiliary fuel pump off. As the engine fires, move the mixture
974 | control to full rich and decrease the throttle to idle.
1036 | Engine mis-starts characterized by sufficient power to take the engine away
1068 | from the starter but dying in 3 to 5 revolutions are the result of an excessively lean
1098 | mixture after the start and can occur in warm or cold temperatures. Repeat the
1129 | starting procedure but allow additional priming time with the auxiliary fuel pump
1160 | switch on “LOW” before cranking is started, or place the auxiliary fuel pump switch
1192 | on “HIGH” immediately for a richer mixture while cranking.
1254 | If prolonged cranking is necessary, allow the starter motor to cool at frequent
1285 | intervals, since excessive heat may damage the armature.
1372 titolo | TAXIING.
1436 | When taxiing, it is important that speed and use of brakes be held to a mini-
1466 | mum and that all controls be utilized (see taxiing diagram, figure 4-2) to maintain
1496 | directional control and balance in strong, gusty winds.
1560 | Taxiing over loose gravel or cinders should be done at low engine speed to avoid
1590 | abrasion and stone damage to the propeller tips and the leading edges of the horizon-
1620 | tal stabilizer.
