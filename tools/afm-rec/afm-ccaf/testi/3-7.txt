702 titolo | RECOVERY FROM A SPIRAL DIVE.
765 | If a spiral is encountered, proceed as follows:
828 | (1) Close the throttle.
857 | (2) Stop the turn by using coordinated aileron and rudder control to align the
888 | symbolic airplane in the turn coordinator with the horizon reference line.
920 | (3) Cautiously apply elevator back pressure to slowly reduce the indicated air-
953 | speed to 145 km/h - 78 kts - 90 MPH.
982 | (4) Adjust the elevator trim control to maintain a 145 km/h - 78 kts - 90 MPH
1018 | glide.
1045 | (5) Keep hands off the control wheel, using rudder control to hold a straight
1079 | heading.
1107 | (6) Clear engine occasionally, but avoid using enough power to disturb the
1140 | trimmed glide.
1169 | (7) Upon breaking out of clouds, apply normal cruising power and resume
1202 | flight.
1322 titolo | ELECTRICAL POWER SUPPLY SYSTEM MALFUNCTIONS.
1383 | Malfunctions in the electrical power supply system can be detected by periodic
1414 | monitoring of the ammeter and over-voltage warning light; however, the cause of
1444 | these malfunctions is usually difficult to determine. Broken or loose alternator wir-
1476 | ing is most likely the cause of alternator failures, although other factors could cause
1507 | the problem. A damaged or improperly adjusted voltage regulator can also cause
1540 | malfunctions. Problems of this nature constitute an electrical emergency and should
1571 | be dealt with immediately. Electrical power malfunctions usually fall into two cate-
1601 | gories: excessive rate of charge and insufficient rate of charge. The paragraphs be-
1632 | low describe the recommended remedy for each situation.
